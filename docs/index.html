/01

your secrets are leaking into six places you forgot about.

Precommit hooks stop secrets from hitting git. They don't stop the secret you pasted into a Claude conversation last Tuesday. That secret is now sitting in plaintext in your home directory, and so is everything that ran after it.

  1. 01 ~/.claude/projects/*.jsonl every Claude turn, verbatim
  2. 02 ~/.codex/sessions/**/rollout-*.jsonl Codex transcripts, dated & archived
  3. 03 ~/.codex/logs_2.sqlite feedback bodies, request payloads
  4. 04 %APPDATA%\Cursor\User\workspaceStorage\ Cursor's chat & file caches
  5. 05 %APPDATA%\Code\User\globalStorage\ Copilot Chat, Augment, Windsurf
  6. 06 ~/Library/Application Support/{Cursor,Windsurf}/ macOS variant of the above

and the things that read your home directory:

  • OS search indexers (Spotlight, Windows Search) — already indexed.
  • Backup tools (Time Machine, OneDrive, iCloud Drive) — already off-disk.
  • SaaS sync clients quietly mirroring ~/Documents upstream.
  • AV / EDR with cloud-upload features for "suspicious files."
  • IT support remote-collection scripts running under your user.
  • The next dev who borrows your unlocked laptop for "five seconds."

tl;dr — your $HOME is not a secret store, but right now it kind of is.

/02

three verbs, all idempotent.

scan

Walks every detected client. Reports findings. Never writes. Safe to run on cron.

redact

Same scan, but rewrites the file in place — atomically — with <redacted by yass:{client}>. Requires --apply to actually mutate. Default is dry-run. Yes, even on this verb.

audit

Every --apply run drops a JSONL log to ~/.yass-audit/. SHA-256 of each redacted secret, no plaintext. That's your rotation worklist.

/05

questions you definitely should ask.

will this break my AI client login?

No. auth.json, credentials.json, token.json, id_token.json, refresh_token.json are explicit no-touch. yass refuses to scan them. You cannot override that — feature, not bug.

is dry-run really the default?

Yes. scan never writes. redact without --apply also never writes. You have to opt in twice (use the redact verb and pass --apply) before anything mutates. This is on purpose.

does redaction = rotation?

No. Absolutely not. Once a secret hit a provider's logs, the bytes may already be exfiltrated. yass cleans up your disk; it cannot un-leak the secret. Read the audit log, group by secret_sha256, and rotate every distinct hash at its provider.

does this cover Slack / Teams / Notion paste history?

Out of scope. yass scopes itself to AI-client local stores (Claude / Codex / Cursor / Windsurf / Augment / Copilot). Chat-app exfil is a separate problem with separate APIs and separate retention policies. If you want to sponsor a sibling project, you know where to find me.

regex? entropy? something cleverer?

Provider-anchored regex only. ~40 high-precision rules (sk-, ghp_, AKIA, thingspat_, JWTs, PEM keys, etc.) plus an allowlist that suppresses ${VAR}, {{var}}, localhost URIs, and other doc placeholders. No entropy heuristics — deliberately, because false positives in transcripts are noisier than misses.
